# Sindh Board / National Curriculum of Pakistan — Knowledge Pack

## Who is who

- **Sindh Textbook Board (STBB), Jamshoro** — writes and publishes the textbooks used in
  Sindh government schools and many low-fee private schools.
- **Bureau of Curriculum and Extension Wing (BCEW), Jamshoro** — curriculum development
  and teacher training for Sindh.
- **Examination boards in Sindh** — BSEK (Karachi), and BISE Hyderabad, Sukkur, Larkana,
  Mirpurkhas, Shaheed Benazirabad (Nawabshah). Also the **Aga Khan University
  Examination Board (AKU-EB)**, an independent national board popular with private
  schools in Sindh, which assesses with a strong emphasis on higher-order questions.
- **Federal Board (FBISE)** — used by federal and many cantonment/garrison schools.

## Curriculum documents

Plans in Sindh are written against **Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs)**. The relevant
documents are the National Curriculum of Pakistan (the 2006 National Curriculum, and the
later National Curriculum of Pakistan 2022–2023 which superseded the Single National
Curriculum branding) together with Sindh's own provincial curriculum work. Adoption
varies by district and school, so **never assert which curriculum edition a school
follows** — write the SLO in plain words and let the teacher map it.

An SLO is phrased: *"Students will be able to ..."* followed by a measurable verb. That
is the same grammar as a lesson objective, so a good plan's objectives read like SLOs
broken into single-lesson bites.

## Stage structure

| Stage | Classes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-primary / Katchi | — | play and readiness based |
| Primary | I – V | class teacher model |
| Elementary / Middle | VI – VIII | subject teachers begin |
| Secondary | IX – X | SSC Part I and II |
| Higher Secondary | XI – XII | HSSC Part I and II |

## Subjects

English, Urdu, **Sindhi** (compulsory in Sindh), Mathematics, General Science (split into
Physics, Chemistry, Biology from Class IX), Islamiat, Pakistan Studies, Social Studies,
Computer Science, and at secondary level optional groups (Science / General / Computer
Science / Commerce).

## What a Sindh Board classroom actually looks like

Plan for it honestly:
- 40 to 60 students is common; 30 is a small class
- Fixed benches, often three to a two-seater
- Blackboard and chalk, or a whiteboard; a projector is the exception
- Textbook is the primary and often only resource in the child's hands
- No printing budget — anything "printed" must have a "or copy onto the board / dictate"
  alternative
- Load-shedding makes any electricity-dependent activity unreliable
- Mixed-ability and mixed-age within one class is normal
- Learners think in Urdu or Sindhi and write in English; translanguaging is a tool, not a
  failure

## Assessment culture

Annual board examinations are marks-based and heavily written. Question stems that
appear repeatedly, and which lessons should rehearse explicitly:

*Define, State, Write short answers, Fill in the blanks, Choose the correct option,
Match column A with column B, Give reasons, Differentiate between, Write a note on,
Solve, Prove, Draw and label, Translate into Urdu, Summarise*

Build the Evaluation section out of these stems so the lesson feeds the exam the child
will actually sit. Include the mark allocation.

## Textbook-tied planning

Sindh teachers plan chapter by chapter from the STBB book. If the teacher named a book
and chapter, use those words verbatim. If they did not, refer to "the prescribed
textbook chapter" and leave page numbers null. Never guess an STBB page number.
