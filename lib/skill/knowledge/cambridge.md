# Cambridge — Curriculum Knowledge Pack

Cambridge Assessment International Education (CAIE). Widely taught in Pakistani private
schools (Beaconhouse, City School, LGS, Roots, Generation's, Bay View, KGS, and many
independent schools).

## Programme ladder

| Programme | Stages | Typical age |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge Primary | Stage 1 – 6 | 5 – 11 |
| Cambridge Lower Secondary | Stage 7 – 9 | 11 – 14 |
| Cambridge Upper Secondary (IGCSE / O Level) | Year 10 – 11 | 14 – 16 |
| Cambridge Advanced (AS & A Level) | Year 12 – 13 | 16 – 19 |

In Pakistani schools Stage N is usually spoken of as "Class N" or "Year N", and O Level
is commonly taken over two or three years (O1/O2/O3).

## How learning objectives are coded

Cambridge Primary and Lower Secondary frameworks code objectives as:

    <stage><strand-letter(s)><sub-strand>.<number>

Examples of the *shape* (illustrative only):
- Mathematics: `4Nc.03` = Stage 4, Number, calculation sub-strand, objective 3
- English: `5Rs.01` = Stage 5, Reading, structure sub-strand, objective 1
- Science: `6Bs.02` = Stage 6, Biology, structure and function, objective 2

**Do not output a specific code unless the teacher gave you one.** Reference the strand
and stage in words instead. The application will render the code field as "not supplied".

## Strands you may reference by name

**Mathematics** — Number; Geometry and Measure; Statistics and Probability.
Plus *Thinking and Working Mathematically* (TWM) characteristics, used in pairs:
Specialising & Generalising, Conjecturing & Convincing, Characterising & Classifying,
Critiquing & Improving. Naming the TWM pair a task develops is a strong plan signal.

**English** — Reading; Writing; Speaking and Listening. (Phonics, spelling and
vocabulary sit inside Reading and Writing at the lower stages.)

**Science** — Biology; Chemistry; Physics; Earth and Space. Plus *Thinking and Working
Scientifically* (TWS): models and representations, scientific enquiry (questions and
methods), practical work (planning, observing, measuring, recording), data analysis,
evaluation.

**Global Perspectives** — Research; Analysis; Evaluation; Reflection; Collaboration;
Communication. Delivered through Challenges rather than content topics.

**Digital Literacy / Computing** — Computational thinking; Programming; Managing data;
Networks and digital communication.

## IGCSE / O Level

Assessment objectives differ per syllabus, but plans should name the paper structure the
lesson feeds (for example "supports Paper 2 structured-response technique"). Command
words matter enormously and should be taught explicitly:

*state, define, describe, explain, suggest, calculate, determine, compare, evaluate,
justify, analyse, discuss, comment on, outline, identify, deduce, predict, sketch*

A good IGCSE plan teaches the difference between *describe* (say what happens) and
*explain* (say why it happens) in the lesson itself.

## Cambridge Approaches to Teaching and Learning

Six pillars — reference these by name in Teaching Methodology where they fit:
1. **Active learning** — learners construct meaning through tasks, not reception
2. **Assessment for learning** — evidence gathered during the lesson changes the teaching
3. **Metacognition** — learners plan, monitor and evaluate their own thinking
4. **Differentiation** — the same objective, varied route
5. **Language awareness** — the subject's language is taught, not assumed (critical in
   Pakistan, where English is an additional language for most learners)
6. **Inclusive education** — barriers removed rather than learners sorted

## Language awareness — required in Pakistani Cambridge classrooms

Most Cambridge learners in Pakistan are EAL. Every plan should pre-teach subject
vocabulary explicitly: put the word on the board, give a learner-friendly definition, a
local example, and one non-example. Allow Urdu for exploratory talk in pair work, then
require English for the written record. Name this in the plan; supervisors look for it.

## House style for Cambridge plans

- Lesson structure commonly follows **5E**: Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate
- Success criteria are written learner-facing ("I can ...") alongside teacher objectives
- Starter and plenary are named as such
- Misconceptions are anticipated in writing before the lesson
